────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PART 1 — WHAT IS THIS APP AND WHY DOES IT EXIST?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

MTN Ghana is a large mobile phone company. Like every big company, it faces risks —
things that can go wrong and hurt its finances. Some examples:

  - The Ghanaian Cedi (GHS) weakens against the US Dollar, making imported equipment
    more expensive.
  - The Bank of Ghana raises interest rates, making it harder to borrow money cheaply.
  - A competitor cuts prices sharply, and MTN loses customers.
  - A new government regulation forces MTN to cut certain fees.

The MTN QuantRisk platform is a dashboard that helps the risk and finance team at MTN
Ghana answer one core question:

  "If something bad happens, how badly hurt would we be — and how likely is that?"

Instead of just guessing or relying on gut feeling, the platform uses real historical
financial data + mathematical models to give numbers, probabilities, and charts.

This is called QUANTITATIVE RISK ANALYSIS — turning "what if?" questions into
actual numbers you can act on.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PART 2 — THE BIG PICTURE: HOW THE APP IS BUILT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The platform has two main pieces that talk to each other:

  ┌─────────────────────────────────┐      ┌──────────────────────────────────┐
  │         FRONTEND                │◄────►│         BACKEND                  │
  │  (what you see in the browser)  │      │  (the engine that does the math) │
  │                                 │      │                                  │
  │  Built with: Next.js + React    │      │  Built with: Python + FastAPI     │
  │  Language: TypeScript           │      │  Language: Python                 │
  │  Runs on: Port 3000             │      │  Runs on: Port 8001               │
  └─────────────────────────────────┘      └──────────────────────────────────┘

Think of it like a restaurant:
  - The FRONTEND is the dining room — beautiful, easy to use, shows you the menu
    and brings you the food.
  - The BACKEND is the kitchen — all the actual cooking (math, models, calculations)
    happens here. You never see it directly.

When you click "Execute Simulation" on the screen, here is what actually happens:

  1. The frontend sends a REQUEST to the backend: "Run scenario S01 at 1.5× severity"
  2. The backend reads the data files, runs the math, and sends back the RESULTS
  3. The frontend takes those numbers and draws the charts and cards you see

This communication uses a system called REST API (a standard way for programs to
talk to each other over the internet).


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PART 3 — THE DATA: WHERE DO THE NUMBERS COME FROM?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The backend reads from several CSV files stored in the "data/structured/" folder:

  base_case.csv
  → The "normal" values for every KPI at the start of FY25 (Financial Year 2025).
    This is the reference point. Everything is compared against this.
    Example row: FIN03 (EBITDA Margin) = 60.2%

  scenario_library.csv
  → A table of every risk scenario and how much it affects each KPI.
    Example row: Scenario S04 "Currency Crisis" → FIN01 (Service Revenue) drops by -8%

  mtn_scenario_library.csv
  → Metadata about each scenario: its name, which risk pillar it belongs to,
    how severe it is (1–5 scale), how plausible it is (1–5 scale).

  annual.csv, quarterly.csv, monthly.csv
  → Historical financial data going back several years.
    Used to train the AI models and to show trend charts.

  macro_context.csv
  → External economic data: inflation rate, Bank of Ghana policy rate,
    GDP growth, Cedi/USD exchange rate, mobile penetration, data penetration.
    These are the INPUTS to the machine learning models.

  segments_annual.csv, operational_annual.csv
  → Segment-level and operational data (MoMo revenue, subscribers, ARPU, etc.)

PART 4 — THE KPIs: WHAT ARE WE TRACKING?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

KPI stands for Key Performance Indicator. These are the 14 numbers the platform
monitors most carefully. They are split into 4 groups:

FINANCIAL KPIs — "How much money is the company making?"
  FIN01 — Service Revenue (unit: GHSm = millions of Ghana Cedis)
           The total money MTN earns from services (calls, data, MoMo, etc.)
           FY25 Base: ~24,000–25,000 GHSm
           Safe range: 23,000 to 26,000 GHSm

  FIN02 — EBITDA (unit: GHSm)
           Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, Amortisation.
           Think of it as "how much money did we make before paying certain costs".
           Safe range: 14,000 to 15,500 GHSm

  FIN03 — EBITDA Margin (unit: %)
           EBITDA divided by Revenue, as a percentage.
           Example: if Revenue = 24,000 and EBITDA = 14,400, then Margin = 60%.
           This tells you how efficiently MTN converts revenue into profit.
           Safe range: 58% to 62%

  FIN04 — PAT (unit: GHSm)
           Profit After Tax — what's left after ALL expenses and taxes.
           Safe range: 7,500 to 8,500 GHSm

  FIN05 — PAT Margin (unit: %)
           PAT as a % of Revenue. Tells you the final profitability.
           Safe range: 31% to 35%

  FIN06 — Revenue Growth YoY (unit: %)
           How much revenue grew compared to last year.
           Safe range: 30% to 40%

SEGMENT KPIs — "Which part of the business is performing?"
  SEG01 — Data Revenue (unit: GHSm)
           Money specifically from internet/data services.
           Safe range: 8,000 to 9,000 GHSm

  SEG03 — MoMo Revenue (unit: GHSm)
           Money from Mobile Money (MoMo) services (like MTN's payment system).
           Safe range: 5,500 to 6,500 GHSm

OPERATIONAL KPIs — "How is the network and customer base doing?"
  OPS01 — Total Subscribers (unit: M = millions)
           Total number of active SIM cards / customers.
           Safe range: 29M to 32M subscribers

  OPS04 — ARPU (unit: GHS)
           Average Revenue Per User — how much each customer pays per month.
           Safe range: 64 to 70 GHS

  OPS07 — 4G Coverage (unit: %)
           Percentage of Ghana covered by 4G network.
           Safe range: 98% to 100%

EXTERNAL KPIs — "What is the economic environment doing?"
  EXT01 — Inflation (unit: %)
           Annual inflation rate in Ghana.
           Note: HIGHER is WORSE. Safe range: 4% to 10%

  EXT02 — BoG Policy Rate (unit: %)
           The Bank of Ghana's benchmark interest rate.
           Note: HIGHER is WORSE. Safe range: 25% to 30%

  EXT03 — Cedi/USD Exchange Rate (unit: GHS per 1 USD)
           How many Cedis you need to buy 1 US Dollar.
           Note: HIGHER is WORSE (more Cedis needed = weaker Cedi).
           Safe range: 11 to 13 GHS/USD

HOW STATUS LEVELS WORK:
For financial KPIs (FIN, SEG, OPS), LOWER values are bad:
  - CRITICAL: value is more than 5% below the lower threshold
  - WARNING:  value is below the lower threshold
  - WATCH:    value is between warning and safe
  - SAFE:     value is comfortably above the lower threshold

For external KPIs (EXT), HIGHER values are bad:
  - CRITICAL: value is above the upper threshold
  - WARNING:  value is above the lower threshold
  - SAFE:     value is below the lower threshold


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PART 5 — THE RISK PILLARS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The 56+ scenarios in the library are grouped into 7 "pillars" — categories of risk:

  A — Macro & FX
      Risks from the economy and exchange rates.
      Examples: "Cedi depreciates 25% against USD", "Inflation spikes to 35%"

  B — Regulatory
      Risks from new government rules or laws.
      Examples: "NCA mandates service price caps", "New mobile money tax introduced"

  C — Tech & Cyber
      Risks from technology failures or cyberattacks.
      Examples: "Core network outage", "Ransomware attack on billing systems"

  D — Competitive
      Risks from competitors gaining market share.
      Examples: "Telecel aggressively cuts data prices", "New competitor enters market"

  E — Operational & Climate
      Risks from internal operations or natural events.
      Examples: "Tower damage from flooding", "Staff strike disrupts operations"

  F — Upside
      Positive scenarios (opportunities, not threats).
      Examples: "Government accelerates broadband rollout", "GDP grows faster than expected"

  G — Tail Risk
      Very severe but unlikely scenarios.
      Examples: "Banking system collapse", "Mass subscriber exodus"

========================================
PAGE 5: PREDICTIVE FORECASTS — /forecasts
========================================

WHAT IT SHOWS:
The app's predictions for where KPIs will go over the next 90 days (approximately
2–3 months into the future).

HOW IT WORKS — THE ARIMA MODEL:
For Service Revenue forecasting, the backend uses an ARIMA model.

WHAT IS ARIMA?
ARIMA stands for Auto-Regressive Integrated Moving Average. That sounds complex,
but here is the simple idea:

Imagine you are tracking revenue every quarter:
  Q1: 5,800  Q2: 6,100  Q3: 5,950  Q4: 6,300  → What will Q5 be?

ARIMA looks at three things:
  1. AUTO-REGRESSIVE (AR): "The value now tends to depend on recent values."
     Example: If revenue was high last quarter, it probably won't crash to zero.
  2. INTEGRATED (I): It first "differences" the data (subtracts one period from
     the previous) to remove long-term trends, making it easier to model patterns.
  3. MOVING AVERAGE (MA): It also looks at past "errors" (how wrong previous
     predictions were) to correct itself.

ARIMA(2,1,1) means:
  - Look back 2 periods for the AR component
  - Difference the data once (the "1" in the middle)
  - Use 1 period of past errors for correction

HOW IT IS TRAINED:
  - The model is trained on historical quarterly revenue data from quarterly.csv
  - Training is done once by running: python models/train_lstm.py
  - The trained model is saved as a file: models/artefacts/arima_revenue.joblib
  - When you view the forecasts page, the backend loads this saved file and asks it
    to predict the next 2–3 quarters

OPTIONAL LSTM MODEL:
There is also an optional LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory neural network) model.
LSTM is a type of AI that is good at learning patterns in sequences of data.
It requires TensorFlow to be installed. If available, it can potentially give better
predictions than ARIMA for complex non-linear patterns. If not available, the system
falls back to ARIMA.

WHAT YOU SEE ON THE PAGE:
  - Select a KPI from the dropdown
  - See a chart with:
    * Historical data (solid line going left)
    * Forecast line (dotted going right) showing median, P05, and P95 bands
  - The SHADED AREA between P05 and P95 is the uncertainty band
    = "we are 90% confident the real value will land in this range"

HOW TO READ THE FORECAST:
  - P50 (median line): Our best single guess — "most likely outcome"
  - P05 (lower edge of shaded area): "Only 5% of the time would it be worse than this"
    = This is your DOWNSIDE RISK floor
  - P95 (upper edge): "Only 5% of the time would it be better than this"
    = This is your UPSIDE ceiling

EXAMPLE:
"The ARPU forecast for next quarter is:
  P05 = 61.2 GHS   (bad case — only 5% chance it's lower)
  P50 = 64.8 GHS   (most likely)
  P95 = 68.1 GHS   (best case — only 5% chance it's higher)
This means management should plan around 64–65 GHS ARPU, but must be prepared
for it dipping as low as 61 GHS in a bad scenario."

========================================
PAGE 7: STRESS TESTER — /scenarios
========================================

This is one of the most important pages. It lets you answer:
"What happens to our KPIs if a specific bad event occurs?"

─────────────────────────
LEFT PANEL: Scenario Library
─────────────────────────
A list of all 56+ scenarios organised by risk pillar.
Click any scenario to load it into the workspace.

Each scenario has:
  - A NAME (e.g., "Cedi/USD Exchange Rate Crisis")
  - A PILLAR (e.g., "Macro & FX")
  - A TYPE (Stress / Shock / Combined / Upside)
  - A SEVERITY RATING (1–5, where 5 = most severe)
  - A PLAUSIBILITY RATING (1–5, where 5 = most likely to happen)
  - KPI IMPACTS (e.g., "FIN01 drops -8%, FIN03 drops -3 percentage points")

─────────────────────────
CENTRE: Simulation Workspace
─────────────────────────

SEVERITY MULTIPLIER SLIDER:
  This scales the scenario's impact up or down.
  - 1.0× = base scenario (as calibrated in the library)
  - 0.5× = half as bad — a mild version of the scenario
  - 2.0× = twice as bad — a severe escalation
  - 2.5× and above = extreme tail-risk territory (shown with a red warning)

MACRO OVERLAYS:
Three additional sliders let you layer extra economic shocks on top of the scenario:
  - Cedi/USD Shock (%): Additional currency depreciation or appreciation
  - Inflation Overlay (pp): Extra percentage points of inflation
  - Policy Rate Overlay (pp): Extra basis points on the Bank of Ghana's rate

WHY MACRO OVERLAYS?
Because real-world crises rarely come alone. A currency crisis might also trigger
higher inflation and force a policy rate increase simultaneously. The overlays let you
model these "compound" scenarios.

─────────────────────────
HOW THE MATH WORKS (Scenario Engine):
─────────────────────────

Step 1: Load the base case
  The backend reads base_case.csv to get the starting value for every KPI.
  Example: FIN01_base = 24,500 GHSm

Step 2: Read the scenario impacts from scenario_library.csv
  Example row: S04, FIN01, pct, -8.0
  This means: "Under scenario S04, Service Revenue changes by -8%"

Step 3: Multiply by the severity multiplier
  If you set severity = 1.5×:
  Actual impact applied = -8.0% × 1.5 = -12.0%

Step 4: Apply the impact to the base value
  Three types of impacts:
  - pct  (percentage change): new_value = base × (1 + impact/100)
    Example: 24,500 × (1 + (-12)/100) = 24,500 × 0.88 = 21,560 GHSm
  - delta (absolute change): new_value = base + impact
    Example: EBITDA Margin 60.2% + (-2.5pp) = 57.7%
  - abs  (set to absolute value): new_value = impact value

Step 5: Apply macro overlays
  If you added a 10% Cedi shock:
  The Cedi/USD rate (EXT03) is also adjusted, AND Service Revenue gets an additional
  small penalty (because a weaker Cedi makes USD-denominated costs more expensive).

Step 6: Compute the delta % for each KPI
  delta_pct = (scenario_value - base_value) / base_value × 100

Step 7: Determine the status (Safe/Watch/Warning/Critical)
  Compares the stressed value against the KPI's threshold bands.

─────────────────────────
RESULTS: What You See
─────────────────────────

NARRATIVE BANNER (at the top of results):
  "Stress Test Complete — Cedi/USD Exchange Rate Crisis
   Scenario applied at 1.5× effective severity across 7 KPIs.
   Average portfolio delta is -4.3% from base values.
   2 KPIs are in critical territory — immediate board-level review is recommended."

This translates all the numbers into a plain-English sentence you can read instantly.

RISK CHIPS (4 boxes: Critical / Warning / Watch / Resilient):
  Shows how many of the 7 tracked KPIs fall into each risk bucket.
  Example: Critical: 2 | Warning: 3 | Watch: 1 | Resilient: 1

MOST IMPACTED KPI panel:
  The single KPI with the largest negative delta is highlighted.
  Example: "EBITDA — Base: 14,300 GHSm → Scenario: 12,150 GHSm  -15.0% vs base"

KPI IMPACT TILES (the grid of cards):
  Each tile shows one KPI with:
  - The base value → scenario value (with an arrow between them)
  - The % change as a coloured chip
  - An interpretation sentence: "EBITDA Margin falls 5.2% — enters warning territory."
  - The status colour (red border = critical, yellow = warning, etc.)

WATERFALL CHARTS:
  These charts decompose the revenue and EBITDA drops into their individual drivers.
  Instead of just saying "Revenue fell 15%", the waterfall shows:
    Base: 24,500
    − Cedi/USD effect:        -2,100
    − Inflation effect:         -800
    − Policy rate effect:       -350
    − GDP growth offset:        +200
    = Stressed: 21,450

  This tells you WHICH factor caused the most damage.

SHAP ATTRIBUTION CHART:
  This uses the XGBoost AI model (explained in Part 7) to rank the features
  by how much they each contributed to the EBITDA Margin change.
  - Yellow/positive bars: this factor HELPS (pushes margin up)
  - Red/negative bars: this factor HURTS (pushes margin down)
  - The longer the bar, the more powerful the effect

EXAMPLE INTERPRETATION:
"We ran scenario S04 (Cedi Crisis) at 1.5×. Service Revenue fell from 24,500 to
21,560 GHSm (-11.9%). EBITDA Margin dropped from 60.2% to 55.1% — entering
critical territory. The SHAP chart shows Cedi/USD was the single biggest driver,
responsible for 38% of the EBITDA Margin decline. This makes sense — MTN has
significant USD-denominated costs for network equipment and roaming agreements."

PAGE 9: REVERSE STRESS — /reverse
========================================

This is the most mathematically sophisticated page. It flips the question around:

Instead of: "If scenario X happens at severity Y, what happens to our KPIs?"
It asks:    "How bad does a scenario need to get before KPI Z breaches its limit?"

─────────────────────────
HOW TO USE IT:
─────────────────────────

STEP 1: Define your "unacceptable outcome" in the Target Builder:
  - Choose the KPI you care about (e.g., EBITDA Margin - FIN03)
  - Choose the threshold type:
    * "Drops below" a specific value (e.g., drops below 55%)
    * "Drops by X%" from base (e.g., drops by 10%)
    * "Rises above" a value (for external KPIs like inflation)
  - Optionally choose a specific scenario to solve for

STEP 2: Choose solve mode:
  - Single Scenario: "How bad does scenario S04 need to get to breach 55% EBITDA Margin?"
  - Cross-Scenario Sweep: "Across ALL scenarios, which ones can breach 55% EBITDA Margin
    and at what severity?"

─────────────────────────
HOW THE MATH WORKS — BINARY SEARCH:
─────────────────────────

The engine uses a technique called BINARY SEARCH to find the exact severity level
that causes the breach. Here is how binary search works:

Imagine you're trying to find the exact floor level in a 10-story building where
the temperature drops below 20°C (you can only check one floor at a time):

  Start: Check floor 5 → too warm (25°C)
  → Must be higher. Check floor 8 → too cold (18°C)
  → Somewhere between 5 and 8. Check floor 6 → perfect (19.9°C)
  → Between 6 and 8. Check floor 7 → just cold (20.1°C)
  → Between 6 and 7. We have our answer: floor 6.5!

The risk engine does the same thing with severity multipliers:

  Start: Does severity = 3.0× breach the threshold? Yes
  → The breach happens somewhere below 3.0×. Try 1.5×
  → Does 1.5× breach? No.
  → Try 2.25× (midpoint between 1.5 and 3.0). Yes.
  → Try 1.875× (midpoint between 1.5 and 2.25). No.
  → Try 2.0625×. Yes.
  → ... keeps halving the gap, 40 times total.
  → Final answer: you need 2.04× severity to breach the threshold.

The app runs the full scenario calculation (from Page 7) at each test point,
so it is doing many simulations inside the binary search.

─────────────────────────
WHAT YOU SEE IN RESULTS:
─────────────────────────

SINGLE SCENARIO SOLVER:
  "Breach Analysis — Cedi/USD Exchange Rate Crisis
   Required Severity Multiplier to breach 55% EBITDA Margin: 2.04×
   At that severity, EBITDA Margin = 54.87%"

  Plus a chart showing the BINARY SEARCH TRAJECTORY:
  Each iteration's (severity, KPI value) point is plotted, showing the search
  converging to the exact breach point. This is like watching the algorithm think.

CROSS-SCENARIO SWEEP TABLE:
  When you run without selecting a specific scenario, the engine runs the binary
  search for EVERY scenario in the library. The results are sorted from most
  dangerous (lowest required severity to cause breach) to least dangerous.

  Example output table:
  Rank | Scenario               | Required Severity | EBITDA at Breach
  ─────┼────────────────────────┼───────────────────┼──────────────────
  1    | Cedi/USD Crisis        | 1.83×             | 54.91%
  2    | Regulatory Fee Caps    | 2.04×             | 54.87%
  3    | Competitor Price War   | 2.31×             | 54.78%
  4    | Tech Outage            | 2.87×             | 54.93%

  Reading this: The Cedi/USD Crisis is the most dangerous scenario for EBITDA Margin
  because it only needs to escalate to 1.83× its calibrated severity to cause a breach.
  A Tech Outage needs 2.87× — you have much more buffer against that.

TOP DANGEROUS SCENARIOS CARD:
  Visual ranking of the top 5 most dangerous scenarios (those needing the lowest
  severity multiplier to cause a breach) shown as a ranked leaderboard.

WHY IS THIS USEFUL?
  This tells you where your BUFFER is. If the Cedi/USD Crisis only needs 1.83×
  severity to breach your EBITDA Margin floor, but you historically see Cedi shocks
  of up to 1.5×, then you have a buffer of 0.33× — relatively thin. You should
  probably hedge your FX exposure.

PAGE 10: MONTE CARLO SIMULATION — /monte-carlo
========================================

This page answers a different kind of question:
"We know the scenario impacts are uncertain. What is the RANGE of possible outcomes,
and how probable is each outcome?"

The Stress Tester gives you ONE answer. Monte Carlo gives you a DISTRIBUTION of answers.

─────────────────────────
THE KEY INSIGHT: Uncertainty
─────────────────────────

When the scenario library says "Cedi Crisis causes -8% Revenue Impact", that -8%
is not a precise number — it is an estimate. The real impact could be -5%, or -12%,
or -9.5%. We do not know exactly.

Monte Carlo embraces this uncertainty by running the simulation THOUSANDS of times,
each time with slightly different impact values, to see all possible outcomes.

─────────────────────────
HOW TO USE IT:
─────────────────────────

You configure 4 parameters:

1. STRESS SCENARIO: Which scenario to simulate (same library as Stress Tester)

2. NUMBER OF SIMULATIONS (N):
   - 200:  Fast (< 1 second). Good for exploration.
   - 500:  Moderate speed, reasonable accuracy.
   - 1000: Balanced — this is the recommended default.
   - 2000: Slow but most accurate percentile estimates.

3. UNCERTAINTY BAND (±%):
   How much can each impact value vary from its stated value?
   - ±5%:  Tight — you trust the impact estimates closely
   - ±20%: Moderate — standard uncertainty assumption (default)
   - ±40%: Wide — very uncertain, many possible outcomes

4. SEVERITY MULTIPLIER:
   Same as in Stress Tester — scales the base scenario magnitude.

─────────────────────────
HOW THE MATH WORKS — MONTE CARLO ENGINE:
─────────────────────────

The engine does this N times (e.g., 1000 times):

  Run #1:
    Take each scenario impact (e.g., FIN01: -8%)
    ADD RANDOM NOISE using a Gaussian (bell curve) distribution:
      perturbed_impact = -8% × (1 + noise)
      where noise is random, centered at 0, with standard deviation = uncertainty_band
      Example: noise = +0.15, so perturbed = -8% × 1.15 = -9.2%
    Apply this perturbed impact to the base case → get stressed KPI value
    Record the result for every KPI.

  Run #2: Same, but a different random noise:
    perturbed = -8% × 0.92 = -7.36%
    → Different stressed value

  Run #3: noise = +0.31 → -8% × 1.31 = -10.48% → Different result again

  ...repeat 1000 times...

  After 1000 runs: Sort all 1000 results for each KPI and calculate PERCENTILES.

WHAT ARE PERCENTILES?
Imagine you ran 1000 races and sorted the finishing times from slowest to fastest.
The 5th percentile (P05) is the time that was slower than 95% of races — the rare,
bad run. The 50th percentile (P50) is right in the middle — the median performance.

Applied to KPIs:
  P05 = "In the worst 5% of our 1000 simulations, the KPI fell to this value"
       This is your RISK FLOOR — the severe downside you need to plan against.
  P25 = "In the worst 25% of simulations, the KPI was at or below this"
       An adverse but more common scenario.
  P50 = "Half of simulations were below this, half above" = THE MEDIAN
       Your best single estimate of what will happen.
  P75 = "In the best 75% of simulations, the KPI exceeded this"
  P95 = "In the best 5% of simulations, the KPI reached this"
       Your BEST CASE ceiling.

  - Runs THOUSANDS of times with varied impact values
  - Gives a distribution: "Revenue most likely falls 10-13%, worst case -18%"
  - Good for: "What is the full range of outcomes, including tail risks?"

Both tools are needed. Use Stress Tester first to understand the scenario, then
Monte Carlo to understand the uncertainty around that outcome.

PART 7 — THE AI MODELS: HOW DOES THE MACHINE LEARNING WORK?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The platform uses three types of machine learning models:

========================================
MODEL 1: XGBoost (for SHAP attributions and KPI prediction)
========================================

WHAT IT DOES:
XGBoost learns the relationship between MACRO ECONOMIC FACTORS and KPI OUTCOMES.
Once trained, it can:
  a) Predict KPI values given a set of macro inputs
  b) Explain WHY it made that prediction (via SHAP)

THE 6 INPUT FEATURES (macro factors):
  1. Inflation_YoY_Pct         — annual inflation rate
  2. Policy_Rate_Pct           — Bank of Ghana policy rate
  3. Cedi_USD_Avg              — average Cedi to Dollar exchange rate
  4. GDP_Growth_Pct            — Ghana GDP growth rate
  5. Mobile_Penetration_Pct    — mobile phone penetration in Ghana
  6. Data_Penetration_Pct      — internet/data penetration in Ghana

THE 6 TARGET KPIs (what it predicts):
  FIN01 — Service Revenue Growth
  FIN03 — EBITDA Margin
  FIN05 — PAT Margin
  SEG03 — MoMo Revenue
  OPS04 — ARPU (Average Revenue Per User)
  DATA  — Data Revenue Growth

HOW XGBOOST WORKS — SIMPLE EXPLANATION:
XGBoost builds a "forest" of decision trees. Each tree is a series of IF-THEN
rules that map inputs to outputs:

  Tree example (very simplified):
  IF Cedi_USD > 13 AND Inflation > 20%
    THEN predict EBITDA_Margin = 55%
  ELSE IF Cedi_USD < 12 AND GDP_Growth > 6%
    THEN predict EBITDA_Margin = 62%
  ELSE
    THEN predict EBITDA_Margin = 59%

XGBoost builds 200 such trees. Each new tree corrects the mistakes of the previous
ones (this is called GRADIENT BOOSTING — each round focuses on the errors).
The final prediction is the average of all 200 trees.

WHY XGBOOST AND NOT SOMETHING SIMPLER (LIKE LINEAR REGRESSION)?
  - XGBoost captures NON-LINEAR relationships. The impact of currency on margins
    is not linear — a small depreciation has a small effect, but beyond a threshold,
    it can compound sharply. XGBoost handles this naturally.
  - XGBoost is robust to outliers and does not require feature scaling to work well
    (though we scale anyway for good practice).
  - It is interpretable via SHAP values (see below).

HOW IT IS TRAINED:
  1. Read the historical data from annual.csv, macro_context.csv, segments_annual.csv,
     operational_annual.csv and MERGE them by year.
  2. Optionally augment with synthetic data points created from the scenario library
     (each scenario is treated as a data point with its stressed outcome values).
  3. Scale the input features using StandardScaler (so all features are on the same
     numerical scale — this helps the model converge).
  4. Train one separate model for each of the 6 target KPIs.
  5. Evaluate using Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (since we have very few real
     data points — typically only 6 years of annual data).
  6. Save each model as a .joblib file in models/artefacts/

LEAVE-ONE-OUT CROSS-VALIDATION — WHY:
With only 6 data points, you cannot split into training/test sets (you'd train
on 5, test on 1). LOO instead trains on 5 and tests on the 6th, then rotates
which one is left out, 6 times. This gives you a fair error estimate despite the
tiny dataset.

SHAP VALUES — HOW THE MODEL EXPLAINS ITSELF:
After making a prediction, SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) decomposes that
prediction into the contribution of each input feature.

SIMPLE EXAMPLE:
Baseline prediction = 60% EBITDA Margin
+ Cedi/USD contribution: -2.1%  (the weak Cedi hurt margins)
+ Inflation contribution: -1.3%  (high inflation raised costs)
+ GDP Growth contribution: +0.8% (good GDP growth helped a bit)
+ Policy Rate contribution: -0.5%
+ Mobile Penetration: +0.1%
+ Data Penetration: +0.2%
= Final prediction: 57.2% EBITDA Margin

This breakdown is what you see in the SHAP Attribution Chart on the Stress Tester page.
Positive SHAP = this factor helped. Negative SHAP = this factor hurt.

The MAGNITUDE (length of the bar) tells you how much — Cedi/USD at -2.1% is
the most important driver in this example.


========================================
MODEL 2: ARIMA (for revenue forecasting)
========================================

(Already explained in Page 5 above — see "Predictive Forecasts")

Key parameters: ARIMA(2,1,1) — looks back 2 periods, differences once, uses 1
period of past errors.
Saved as: models/artefacts/arima_revenue.joblib
Used on: /forecasts page


========================================
MODEL 3: LSTM (optional neural network forecaster)
========================================

WHAT IT IS:
Long Short-Term Memory network — a type of Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)
designed specifically for sequences of data (like time series).

HOW IT WORKS:
Unlike ARIMA (which uses a fixed formula), LSTM learns complex patterns from data.
It has "memory cells" that can remember important past values and forget
irrelevant ones — this makes it powerful for long-range dependencies.

Architecture used here:
  Layer 1: LSTM with 64 units (learns broad patterns)
  Layer 2: Dropout 20% (prevents overfitting — randomly ignores 20% of connections during training)
  Layer 3: LSTM with 32 units (refines patterns)
  Layer 4: Dropout 20%
  Layer 5: Dense(1) output layer (produces the forecast)

Training approach:
  - Uses a "lookback window" of 4 quarters (looks at the last 4 quarters to predict the next)
  - Trains for 50 epochs using Adam optimizer
  - Requires TensorFlow to be installed

WHY BOTH ARIMA AND LSTM:
  - ARIMA is reliable, fast, and interpretable. It is the primary model.
  - LSTM can potentially learn more complex patterns but needs more data to work well.
  - With only 6–7 years of quarterly data (~24–28 data points), ARIMA is often better.
  - LSTM is included as an optional upgrade path as more data accumulates.

PART 9 — HOW TO INTERPRET RESULTS: A COMPLETE EXAMPLE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Let's walk through a complete example from start to finish.

SCENARIO: "Ghana Currency Shock" (S04 — Cedi/USD Exchange Rate Crisis)
The Cedi depreciates sharply. The library says this causes:
  FIN01 (Service Revenue): -8% impact
  FIN02 (EBITDA):          -5% impact
  FIN03 (EBITDA Margin):   -3pp delta
  FIN04 (PAT):             -7% impact
  FIN05 (PAT Margin):      -2pp delta
  EXT03 (Cedi/USD):        +15% (Cedi weakens = rate rises)

BASE CASE (from base_case.csv):
  FIN01 = 24,500 GHSm
  FIN02 = 14,300 GHSm
  FIN03 = 60.2%
  FIN04 =  8,100 GHSm
  FIN05 = 33.1%
  EXT03 = 11.6 GHS/USD (safe range: 11–13)

────────────────────────────
STRESS TESTER (severity = 1.5×):
────────────────────────────

Actual impacts applied (multiplied by 1.5):
  FIN01: -8% × 1.5 = -12%    → 24,500 × 0.88 = 21,560 GHSm  → delta = -11.99%
  FIN02: -5% × 1.5 = -7.5%   → 14,300 × 0.925 = 13,228 GHSm → delta = -7.50%
  FIN03: -3pp × 1.5 = -4.5pp → 60.2 - 4.5 = 55.7%           → delta = -4.5pp
  FIN04: -7% × 1.5 = -10.5%  → 8,100 × 0.895 = 7,249 GHSm  → delta = -10.50%
  FIN05: -2pp × 1.5 = -3pp   → 33.1 - 3.0 = 30.1%           → delta = -3.0pp
  EXT03: +15% × 1.5 = +22.5% → 11.6 × 1.225 = 14.21 GHS/USD → CRITICAL (above 13)

Status checks:
  FIN01 = 21,560 GHSm vs lower threshold 23,000 → CRITICAL (21,560 < 23,000 × 0.95 = 21,850)
  FIN02 = 13,228 GHSm vs lower threshold 14,000 → CRITICAL (13,228 < 14,000 × 0.95 = 13,300)
    Actually 13,228 < 13,300 = CRITICAL
  FIN03 = 55.7% vs lower threshold 58% → CRITICAL (55.7 < 58 × 0.95 = 55.1). Borderline.
    55.7 > 55.1 so actually WARNING (below lower threshold, not below 95% of it)
  FIN04 = 7,249 GHSm vs lower threshold 7,500 → WARNING (7,249 < 7,500 but > 7,125)
  FIN05 = 30.1% vs lower threshold 31% → WARNING
  EXT03 = 14.21 GHS/USD vs upper threshold 13 → CRITICAL (higher is worse)

Result summary:
  Critical: 3 (FIN01, FIN02, EXT03)
  Warning: 3 (FIN03, FIN04, FIN05)
  Watch: 0
  Resilient: 1 (other KPIs not impacted)

The narrative banner would say:
  "Stress Test Complete — Ghana Currency Shock
   Applied at 1.5× severity across 7 KPIs.
   Average portfolio delta: -7.5% from base.
   3 KPIs are in critical territory — immediate board-level review is recommended."

────────────────────────────
MONTE CARLO (1000 simulations, ±20% uncertainty):
────────────────────────────

For EBITDA Margin, after 1000 runs with random noise on the -4.5pp impact:
  Some runs produce -3.8pp, some -5.2pp, some -4.9pp...

  Sort all 1000 outcomes:
  P05 = 52.3%  (5% of sims were at or below 52.3%)
  P25 = 54.8%  (25% of sims were at or below 54.8%)
  P50 = 55.7%  (median — matches our stress tester result closely)
  P75 = 56.6%  (75% of sims were at or below 56.6%)
  P95 = 57.9%  (95% of sims were at or below 57.9%)

Interpretation sentence:
  "In the worst 5% of 1,000 simulated outcomes, EBITDA Margin could fall by 12.4%
   to 52.3%. The median outcome sits at 55.7%, while the most favourable 5% of
   simulations see it reach 57.9% (-0.5% below base — still not quite recovering)."

Key takeaway: Even in the BEST 5% of simulations, EBITDA Margin is still below the
58% safe threshold. This means there is essentially no scenario where this currency
shock leaves EBITDA Margin unscathed. The board should prepare mitigation measures.

────────────────────────────
REVERSE STRESS (target: EBITDA Margin drops below 55%):
────────────────────────────

Binary search finds: Required severity = 1.37×
Interpretation:
  "The Ghana Currency Shock only needs to escalate to 1.37× its calibrated severity
   before EBITDA Margin breaches the 55% floor. Given that the scenario's baseline
   calibration corresponds to a moderate Cedi depreciation, a 1.37× event is
   considered plausible. Management should have a contingency plan ready."

Cross-Scenario ranking shows this scenario is the 2nd most dangerous for EBITDA Margin
across all 56 scenarios — only the "Combined Macro Shock" scenario is more dangerous
(requiring just 1.21× severity to breach the same threshold).

PART 10 — GLOSSARY OF ALL TECHNICAL TERMS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ARIMA — Auto-Regressive Integrated Moving Average. A statistical model for forecasting
time series data. Uses past values and past errors to predict future values.

ARPU — Average Revenue Per User. Total revenue divided by number of subscribers.
A higher ARPU means each customer is paying more on average.

Binary Search — A mathematical technique for finding an exact value by repeatedly
halving a search range until the answer is found. Used in Reverse Stress to find
the exact breach severity.

BoG — Bank of Ghana. Ghana's central bank, which sets the policy interest rate.

Calibration — The process of adjusting a scenario's impact values to match historical
data or expert judgement. "Calibrated to FY22" means the impacts are based on what
actually happened during 2022.

Cedi (GHS) — Ghana's currency. GHSm means millions of Ghana Cedis.

Cross-Validation — A technique for testing model accuracy when you have limited data.
Leave-One-Out (LOO) cross-validation tests each data point once by training without it.

Delta — The difference between two values. "A delta of -8%" means the value fell by 8%.

Distribution — The spread of all possible outcomes. A distribution tells you both the
likely outcome AND how much variation to expect.

Downside Risk — How bad things could get in a negative scenario.

EBITDA — Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortisation. A measure
of a company's core operating profitability, before financial/accounting adjustments.

Epoch — One full pass through all training data during neural network training.
"50 epochs" means the neural network saw all the data 50 times.

FX — Foreign Exchange. "FX risk" means risk from currency rate changes.

Gaussian Noise — Random variations that follow a bell curve (normal distribution).
Most noise is small and close to zero; very large noise values are rare.

GDP — Gross Domestic Product. The total economic output of a country. A growth proxy.

IQR — Interquartile Range. The range from P25 to P75. The middle 50% of outcomes.
This is where "typical" outcomes land — not too good, not too bad.

KPI — Key Performance Indicator. A number the company tracks to measure performance.

KRI — Key Risk Indicator. A KPI specifically focused on measuring or signalling risk.

LSTM — Long Short-Term Memory. A type of neural network designed for learning from
sequences of data. Has "memory cells" that can retain important historical patterns.

Macro Overlay — An additional shock layered on top of a scenario. E.g., adding
"Cedi depreciates another 10%" on top of a "Competitor Price War" scenario.

Monte Carlo — A simulation technique that runs thousands of scenarios with random
variations to produce a probability distribution of outcomes. Named after the casino
in Monaco because it uses randomness (like gambling).

NCA — National Communications Authority. Ghana's telecom regulator.

Neural Network — A machine learning model inspired by the human brain. Consists of
layers of interconnected mathematical "neurons" that learn patterns from data.

Overfitting — When a model learns the training data too perfectly and fails to
generalise to new data. Prevented by Dropout layers and regularisation.

PAT — Profit After Tax. The final "bottom line" profit after all deductions.

Percentile — A value that separates a data set at a given percentage.
P05 = 5th percentile = only 5% of values are below this.
P50 = 50th percentile = median = exactly half are above, half below.
P95 = 95th percentile = 95% of values are below this.

Pillar — One of the 7 categories of risk: Macro & FX, Regulatory, Tech & Cyber,
Competitive, Operational & Climate, Upside, Tail Risk.

Plausibility — How likely a scenario is to occur in reality. Rated 1–5 (5 = very likely).

Random Seed — A starting number for a random number generator. Using the same seed
produces the same "random" sequence, making simulations reproducible.

REST API — Representational State Transfer Application Programming Interface.
A standardised way for software programs to communicate over the internet using
HTTP requests (GET, POST, PUT, DELETE).

Reverse Stress Testing — Finding the severity level at which a given outcome (like
breaching a KPI threshold) would occur. The opposite of regular stress testing.

SHAP — SHapley Additive exPlanations. A technique from game theory adapted for AI.
It assigns each input feature a "contribution score" explaining how much it influenced
a prediction.

Severity Multiplier — A scaling factor applied to a scenario's impact values.
1.0× = calibrated base severity. 2.0× = twice as severe. 0.5× = half as severe.

Sparkline — A tiny line chart shown within a table or card. Shows the general trend
direction without taking up much space.

Standard Deviation (Std) — A measure of how spread out values are. Higher std =
more uncertainty. In Monte Carlo, a KPI with high std has more variable outcomes.

Stress Testing — Applying an adverse scenario to financial data to see how badly it
would impact key metrics. A standard requirement for financial institutions.

Tail Risk — Very severe but unlikely events. The "tail" of a probability distribution.
Scenarios in the G pillar are tail risks.

TreeExplainer — The specific SHAP algorithm used with XGBoost (tree-based) models.
It efficiently computes exact SHAP values for decision trees.

Waterfall Chart — A chart that shows how a final value is built up from or broken down
into components. Shows the contribution of each driver to a total change.

XGBoost — eXtreme Gradient Boosting. An ensemble machine learning algorithm that
builds many decision trees sequentially, each correcting the previous one's errors.
Known for high accuracy on tabular data and fast computation.

YoY — Year on Year. A comparison of the current period against the same period last year.

PART 13 — FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Q: The numbers don't seem real. Are they real MTN data?
A: The base_case.csv contains representative FY25 figures for MTN Ghana. Some values
   may be rounded or slightly adjusted for confidentiality. The models and scenarios
   are calibrated to be directionally accurate, not necessarily the exact published
   figures.

Q: Why does the Forecasts page sometimes not load?
A: The ARIMA model must be trained first. Run: python models/train_lstm.py
   If the model file doesn't exist at models/artefacts/arima_revenue.joblib,
   the API will return an error.

Q: Why does the SHAP chart show the same values every time?
A: The SHAP calculation runs on the trained XGBoost model at the default macro values.
   If the model hasn't been trained, the backend uses heuristic fallback values.
   Train the model to get real SHAP values.

Q: What does "calibrated to trailing 36-month internal shock bounds" mean?
A: The scenario impact values were set based on looking at what actually happened to
   MTN's KPIs over the past 3 years of historical data. The range of shocks observed
   in that period defines the "calibration bounds".

Q: How accurate is the ARIMA forecast?
A: With ~6 years of annual data and ~24 quarters of quarterly data, the ARIMA model
   has limited data to learn from. Treat the forecast as directional guidance, not
   a precise prediction. The uncertainty bands (P05–P95) reflect this.

Q: Can I add my own scenarios?
A: Yes. On the Stress Tester page, click "+" next to the scenario library to open
   the Create Scenario form. Fill in the KPI impacts, severity, and plausibility.
   The new scenario is saved to scenario_library.csv and immediately available.

Q: Why does Monte Carlo sometimes give slightly different results when run twice?
A: Monte Carlo uses random number generation. If you don't set a fixed random seed,
   each run produces slightly different results. Run 1000+ simulations to get
   stable percentile estimates.

Q: What is the difference between "severity" and "severity multiplier"?
A: "Severity" (rated 1–5) is a qualitative rating of how serious a scenario is.
   "Severity Multiplier" (0.5× to 3.0×) is the numerical scaling factor you set
   when running a simulation to scale the impacts up or down.

Q: What is a "good" EBITDA Margin for MTN Ghana?
A: MTN Ghana historically targets 58–62%. A margin below 58% triggers a Warning;
   below 55.1% (5% below the lower threshold) triggers Critical. Global telecom
   companies typically have EBITDA margins between 35–60%, so MTN Ghana's targets
   are ambitious and reflect its strong market position.

PART 14 — THE NEWS FEED PAGE (/news)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS
The News Feed is a live intelligence layer that automatically scrapes 12 Ghana
and Africa news sources every 15 minutes, runs every article through an AI
pipeline, and shows you which ones are relevant to MTN Ghana's risk exposure.

WHAT IT SHOWS YOU
Each article card displays:
  · Source name and time (e.g. "JoyFM · 2 hours ago")
  · Risk tier stripe on the left edge — red for Critical, amber for Warning,
    yellow for Watch, grey for unscored
  · Risk category badge (Regulatory / FX-Financial / Competitive / Operational /
    Political / Reputational)
  · Sentiment chip (Positive / Neutral / Negative) from the FinBERT AI model
  · GHS impact estimate: "GHS 45M – 375M" shows you the potential financial
    exposure range if the event described actually unfolds fully

HOW TO READ THE FILTER CHIPS
Click any chip at the top to filter by risk category. "All" shows everything.
"Regulatory" shows only articles about NCA fines, spectrum licences, compliance.
"FX-Financial" shows only Cedi/dollar movement articles.

CLICK TO EXPAND
Click any article card to open the full-body drawer on the right. Inside you see:
  · Full article text
  · Named entities: Organisations, Money amounts, Locations, Persons
  · All keyword hit counts per category (e.g. regulatory: 5 hits)
  · A link to the original article on the source website

SCRAPE NOW BUTTON
Click "Scrape Now" in the top bar to manually trigger a new scrape cycle.
The backend fetches new articles from all 12 sources immediately instead of
waiting for the next 15-minute APScheduler tick.

SUMMARY BAR
The three tiles at the top show:
  · Articles today — how many were published and scraped since midnight
  · Top risk category — which category has the most articles today
  · Total articles — cumulative count in the database

PART 15 — THE ALERTS PAGE (/alerts)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT AN ALERT IS
When the NLP pipeline scores an article, it calculates a severity score (0–10)
and an MTN relevance score (0–1). If both scores are high enough, the pipeline
automatically creates an alert record. Alerts are the system's way of saying:
"This article is not just interesting — it could actually hurt MTN Ghana."

ALERT TIERS
  CRITICAL  — severity ≥ 7.5 AND MTN relevance ≥ 0.25
              Examples: NCA license suspension, Cedi crash > 15%, major cyber breach
  WARNING   — severity ≥ 5.0 AND MTN relevance ≥ 0.25
              Examples: New mobile money tax bill, competitor price war
  WATCH     — severity ≥ 3.0 AND MTN relevance ≥ 0.25
              Examples: Minor regulatory update, BOG rate adjustment

The relevance gate (≥ 0.25) means that a general-interest Africa story (even
if dramatic) will not trigger an alert unless it specifically mentions MTN,
Ghana, MoMo, or related entities.

WHAT THE CARDS SHOW
Each alert card shows:
  · Coloured left stripe: red = Critical, amber = Warning, yellow = Watch
  · Category badge and severity score (e.g. "Regulatory · 8.2/10")
  · GHS impact estimate: the mid-point financial exposure in Ghana Cedis
  · MTN relevance score: 0.0 to 1.0 (higher = more directly about MTN Ghana)
  · Time the alert was created
  · Acknowledge button

HOW ACKNOWLEDGE WORKS
Click "Acknowledge" and the alert is immediately greyed out. The UI updates
instantly (optimistic update) while the API call happens in the background.
Acknowledged alerts are still stored in the database — they just move to the
"acknowledged" filter view. Nothing is deleted.

FILTER CHIPS
  All Tiers / Critical / Warning / Watch — filter by tier
  All / Unacknowledged — filter by acknowledge status

THE SUMMARY ROW
The four tiles at the top show: Total Active · Critical count · Warning count
· Watch count. These are the same numbers shown on the Dashboard intelligence strip.

PART 16 — GHANA MACRO DASHBOARD (/economics)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS
The Ghana Macro Dashboard fetches live macroeconomic data for Ghana directly
from the World Bank Open Data API. No API key is required — the World Bank
provides this data completely free of charge. Results are cached for 6 hours
to avoid hammering the API.

THE SIX INDICATORS

1. CPI INFLATION (%)
   Consumer Price Index growth year-on-year. Ghana's inflation is structurally
   high — anything above 12% triggers a Warning signal in the system.
   Why it matters: High inflation raises MTN's operating costs (staff, energy,
   rent) and erodes real ARPU (what customers actually pay in purchasing power).

2. GDP GROWTH (%)
   Ghana's annual real GDP growth. Negative growth triggers a Critical signal.
   Why it matters: GDP growth drives subscriber acquisition and ARPU growth.
   A recession means fewer people can afford data bundles.

3. GHS / USD EXCHANGE RATE
   How many Ghana Cedis per US Dollar (annual average). A rising number means
   the Cedi is weakening — a direct cost increase for MTN Ghana because most
   network equipment and Group intercompany charges are USD-denominated.

4. UNEMPLOYMENT RATE (%)
   Ghana's unemployment as a percentage of the labor force.
   Why it matters: High unemployment suppresses consumer spending on
   telecommunications and mobile money usage.

5. PUBLIC DEBT (% of GDP)
   Central government debt as a share of GDP. When this is high, Ghana's
   government may impose emergency levies (like the 2022 e-levy) to raise
   revenue, directly hitting MTN's mobile money business.

6. FDI NET INFLOWS (% of GDP)
   Foreign direct investment flowing into Ghana. Rising FDI is a positive
   signal — it means international investors believe in the Ghana economy,
   which typically supports a stronger Cedi and growth environment.

READING THE SPARKLINES
Each indicator card shows a small sparkline (trend line) in the bottom-right
corner. Green line = improving trend; red line = deteriorating trend.
The direction is calibrated to what's GOOD for MTN:
  · Inflation going down  = green (lower costs)
  · GDP growth going up   = green (more customers)
  · GHS/USD going down    = green (stronger Cedi)

RISK CONTEXT BANNER
The banner at the top of the page (and on the Dashboard) summarises the two
most important risk signals:
  "Ghana inflation 14.2% | GDP growth 6.0% | GHS/USD 12.56"
  "Inflation: Warning | Growth: Normal"

These signals feed directly into the scenario engine — when inflation is at
Warning level, the FX/Financial stress scenarios become more relevant.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PART 17 — THE AI PIPELINE: HOW AN ARTICLE BECOMES AN ALERT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Here is the full journey of an article from source to dashboard alert:

STEP 1 — SCRAPING (every 15 minutes)
  APScheduler fires → scraper_service.py fetches 12 RSS feeds using feedparser
  Optional: GNews API adds targeted "MTN Ghana telecom" search results
  Each article → { url, title, body, source_name, published_at }

STEP 2 — DEDUPLICATION
  Before saving, the system checks: is this URL already in the database?
  If yes → skip. SQLite UNIQUE constraint on URL handles race conditions.

STEP 3 — MTN RELEVANCE GATE
  compute_mtn_relevance(title + body) → score 0.0 to 1.0
  MTN keywords score 0.35 each; Ghana keywords score 0.10 each.
  If score < 0.2 → article is irrelevant; skip NLP pipeline entirely.
  This keeps the database clean — a cricket score from BBC Africa won't be scored.

STEP 4 — RISK CATEGORY CLASSIFICATION
  Two methods run simultaneously:
    a) Keyword matching: count hits per category across ~80 curated keywords
    b) Zero-shot AI (if HF_TOKEN is set): facebook/bart-large-mnli classifies
       the article into one of 6 categories via HuggingFace Inference API
  If AI is confident (>55%), it overrides the keyword result.
  Result: one of { regulatory, fx_financial, competitive, operational,
                   political, reputational }

STEP 5 — SENTIMENT (FinBERT)
  If HF_TOKEN env var is set:
    → POST to api-inference.huggingface.co/models/ProsusAI/finbert
    → Financial-domain sentiment model, trained on financial news
    → Returns: positive / neutral / negative with confidence score
  If no token:
    → Lexicon fallback: count positive/negative word lists
  Result: { sentiment, sentiment_confidence }

STEP 6 — SEVERITY SCORING
  severity = keyword_score × 1.5 (capped at 10.0)
  If zero-shot AI was used: severity = keyword_score × 0.6 + (AI_confidence × 10) × 0.4
  confidence = min(1.0, keyword_hits × 0.15 + 0.2)

STEP 7 — GHS IMPACT ESTIMATE
  mid = base_exposure[category] × (severity / 10) × mtn_relevance × max(confidence, 0.2)
  Base exposures per category (GHS millions):
    fx_financial: 200  |  regulatory: 120  |  competitive: 80
    operational:   60  |  political:   50  |  reputational: 40
  min = mid × 0.30,  max = mid × 2.50

STEP 8 — ALERT TIER DECISION
  mtn_relevance ≥ 0.25 required (gate — no alerts for tangential articles)
  severity ≥ 7.5 → Critical
  severity ≥ 5.0 → Warning
  severity ≥ 3.0 → Watch
  else           → no alert (article is scored but not alerted)

STEP 9 — STORAGE
  RiskScore row saved to SQLite linked to the Article
  Alert row created if tier is not None
  Both appear in /news and /alerts immediately

TOTAL PIPELINE LATENCY: < 3 seconds per article (without AI model calls)
                        < 15 seconds per article (with HF Inference API)

PART 18 — MOBILE APP (EXPO GO)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The mobile app runs in Expo Go on iOS and Android. It calls the same FastAPI
backend as the web dashboard. No separate mobile API exists.

HOW TO RUN IT
1. Install Expo Go from the App Store / Google Play
2. Make sure the FastAPI backend is running: uvicorn app.main:app --port 8001
3. Make sure your phone is on the same WiFi as your laptop
4. Run: npx expo start (in the mobile/ folder)
5. Scan the QR code with Expo Go (iOS) or the Expo Go app (Android)

THE FOUR TABS

DASHBOARD (Home)
  Shows all 14 KPIs grouped by category: Financial, Segment, Operational,
  External. Each KPI tile shows the FY25 value, unit, and status colour
  (green = on-track, yellow = at-risk, red = off-track). Critical KPIs have
  a highlighted border. Pull down to refresh.

NEWS
  Live article feed from all scraped sources. Tap a category chip to filter.
  Each article shows source, time, category badge, and the risk tier colour
  stripe. Pull down to refresh. "Scrape" button in top-right triggers a manual
  scrape cycle.

ALERTS
  Active risk alerts sorted by severity. Summary bar shows counts per tier.
  Tap "Acknowledge" to dismiss an alert from the active list. The item
  immediately disappears from the unacknowledged view.

ABOUT (System Status)
  Shows the backend pipeline health check (all data sources green/red),
  live Ghana macro signals from World Bank, quick links to the API docs,
  and team/project information.

CONNECTING TO PRODUCTION
  If the app is deployed to Railway/Render, change API_BASE in each screen
  file from 'http://127.0.0.1:8001' to your production URL. This is the only
  change needed to switch the app from local to production mode.
